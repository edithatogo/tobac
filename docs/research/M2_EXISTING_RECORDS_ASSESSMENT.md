## Administrative records

The [official survey access page](https://www.health.govt.nz/monitoring-statistics/surveys/access-to-survey-microdata)
(last updated 6 November 2025) identifies CURFs managed by Stats NZ and continuous
NZHS core data in the IDI. It explicitly excludes module data from that IDI route;
linkage depends on consent and controlled researcher/project access. Its table lists
older survey vintages, so the public 2024/25 results do not prove current microdata
availability. The [2024/25 content guide landing page](https://www.health.govt.nz/publications/questionnaires-and-content-guide-202425-new-zealand-health-survey)
identifies an adult tobacco/vaping module. The [official showcards](https://www.health.govt.nz/system/files/2025-11/nz-health-survey-2024-25-adult-showcards.pdf),
TOB3.02a, contain quit-attempt support categories. This is evidence of questionnaire
content, not respondent answers, successful cessation, illicit origin or cap effects.

Therefore existing survey records are a plausible source of product use, intensity
and quit-history detail, subject to vintage-specific dictionary verification. Do not
assume module variables are in the IDI, individual respondents form a longitudinal
panel, or broad regions identify sewage catchments. Required access decisions concern
exact vintages, fields, geography, dates, design weights and permissible linkage.
They precede any decision to commission another survey.

Existing [2025 returns](https://www.health.govt.nz/regulation-legislation/smoked-tobacco/information-for-manufacturers-and-importers/tobacco-returns/tobacco-returns-2025)
and retained reconciliation remain lawful-release records, not consumed resident
volume. The public summary does not settle the BAT heading-year and R&S pack/carton
conflicts. Preserve native-unit alternatives. Source-owner clarification is conditional:
it is unnecessary if a conclusion is robust to both interpretations. Matched stock,
visitor/duty-free and local-period allocations still need records, not zero defaults.

[Customs interception metadata](https://www.customs.govt.nz/about-us/statistics/non-drug-interceptions)
distinguishes intercepted, detained and legally seized goods. Some detained goods
can subsequently be released. Its extraction/revision dates must travel with the
series. This resolves an endpoint-definition issue: do not label every interception
an illicit seizure or consumption. Counts still depend on enforcement/detection and
do not identify incremental organised-crime harm. Existing incident and effort
records would need a credible counterfactual; national totals cannot supply one.

## Laboratory records

The [NZ wastewater study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2025.181223)
reports 142 samples, 12 treatment plants and nine settlements, May–October 2023,
with paired weekday/weekend sampling. It locates per-day/site metabolite loads in
Tables S13/S15 and figures S22/S23; data are available on request. These repeated
baseline observations already exist. They are not observations of the unimplemented
600-retailer policy. Derived loads reuse flow/population and calibration inputs;
obtain native concentration/censoring, sample/batch pairing, QC, flow and denominator
records before covariance fitting. Published ANBA quantification is sparse; a larger
number of existing specimens does not repair poor near-limit quantification.

The [human anabasine study](https://doi.org/10.1093/ntr/ntag042) also states that data
are available from the authors on request. Its selected cigarette-user yield subset
is useful existing validation, not a target-wide product/absorption factor. Obtain
raw paired measurements and calibration dependencies before considering additional
participants. Neither request statement supplies access approval or actual raw files.

The R05 proposed LOQ of 2 ng/L original influent is a procurement target, not a
published method qualification. New laboratory validation is necessary only if
existing sample/batch records cannot establish adequate original-matrix performance,
recovery, stability, product mixture and vaping contributions for the selected target.
Stored aliquot reanalysis may suffice if availability and storage stability are verified;
new field collection is not the automatic first step.

## Financial and implementation records

The [2023 regime Cabinet release](https://www.health.govt.nz/system/files/2023-12/cabinet_material_establishing_the_smoked_tobacco_regulatory_regime_black_box_watermarked.pdf)
records Budget 2022 funding of NZD30.7 million over four years for regime establishment
and operation. Indexed paragraphs 53–55 distinguish application fees from operating
activities not recovered through those fees. This is a later historical scope than
the 2021 RIS benchmark. Do not add them as independent costs, treat funding as actual
expenditure, divide indiscriminately by retailers or use either as a current study quote.

The [2024 repeal Cabinet release](https://www.health.govt.nz/system/files/2024-02/options_to_repeal_amendments_to_smokefree_env_.act_assoc_regs_black_box_pr.pdf),
paragraph 20 / PDF page 4, records 439 paid applications at NZD1475 plus GST and
334 completed but unpaid at suspension on 1 December 2023. The implied paid amount
is NZD647525 excluding GST, or NZD744653.75 with an explicit 15% GST arithmetic
assumption, consistent with the source's over-NZD744000 description. These are
historical application receipts, not approved/operating outlets, labour/resource costs,
or a complete retailer census. The same release's PDF page 43 notes ESR product
nicotine-testing methodology work; product compliance testing does not validate
wastewater anabasine recovery or human excretion.

Actual administrative activity and financial records therefore existed. Custodian-held
application-processing time, invoices, staffing and refund records could inform
bottom-up costs, but their existence as accessible line items is not verified here.
For a selected current study, quantities and same-specimen panel/validation prices
remain necessary; general fees, lost excise and sales revenue are unsuitable substitutes.
Fees/taxes require an explicit decision perspective to avoid counting transfers as
additional social resource consumption.

## Residual requirements after assessment

1. Human selection of a deliverable contrast, population, period and comparator;
   or an explicit choice to retain hypothetical, conditional research. Historical
   applications and baseline observations cannot create an implemented policy.
2. Custodian decisions/access to the necessary existing raw survey, laboratory,
   local supply/stock/visitor and incident/effort records. Confirm vintage, geography,
   permissions and linkage first. Resolve source year/unit conflicts only if material.
3. Additional validation or observations only for fields/target performance that those
   records cannot support: near-limit accuracy, product/yield/vaping transport, shared
   errors and held-out outcomes. No specific new sample count is justified yet.
4. Matched behavioural, replacement/mitigation and incremental-harm outcomes with a
   credible counterfactual if an empirical causal claim is required. Suitable existing
   linked records may suffice for an actually implemented contrast. Expert elicitation
   requires human responses and produces conditional judgments, not field validation.
5. Current target-specific resource quantities, prices and adoption/timing from actual
   accounts or supplier estimates; obtain new cost information only for missing items.
6. Māori governance, permissible use/privacy, equitable coverage and normative
   health/crime/cost trade-offs. Public record availability does not decide these.
