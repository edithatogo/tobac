## Methods and dependence

Original Methods, subsection Data Analysis, equations
1–6, distinguish total nicotine metabolites from anabasine-based tobacco estimates.
The latter introduces an excretion factor, cigarette nicotine dose, tobacco content,
population extrapolation and remoteness weights. These are method-specific assumptions
and measurements with different scope; do not apply the earlier aggregate-nicotine
non-identification argument as a claim that this paper measured no tobacco-specific
marker. Conversely, anabasine alone does not identify lawful versus illicit origin.

Equation 6 subtracts legal tobacco estimates obtained from an industry-funded FTI
report. Preserve the FTI source, international-arrival assumptions, legal-sales basis,
stock/timing and uncertainty separately. The resulting illicit series is a derived
accounting estimate, not an independently assayed origin observation. Its covariance
with legal sales and other NWDMP-derived outputs cannot be assumed zero. Do not
condition on this derived series and all its component inputs independently.

Supplement 1 eMethods describes catchment population interpolation and sampled-flow
normalisation; eTables 1–2 describe different analyte sampling/pooling designs and
coverage. Do not carry the total-nicotine catchment denominator into anabasine results
without matching the actual sampling occasion. The Discussion's limitations identify
excretion heterogeneity, incomplete anabasine analysis and inability to separate vaping
from medicinal NRT. Those limitations matter for NZ transport and uncertainty.

Equation images 4–5 were reopened visually; source text and tables were inspected.
This is partial formula appraisal. Full conversion/uncertainty propagation, all source
coefficients and supplementary figures still require an external extraction ledger
and independent dimensional/reference checks before any parameter adoption.

request to researchers with approved proposed use and investigator support. No
public raw-data download is supplied there. Under the existing public-existing-data-only
scope, record raw-data access as unavailable; send no request. Public article tables,
figures and methods remain separate candidates for verified summary-level extraction.

The paper supplies neither current NZ assays nor a transported NZ causal policy
response, a complete programme-cost model or an empirical wastewater EVSI/ENBS result.
The next task is source-level extraction and dependence appraisal, with the quarantined
join excluded from accepted inputs until resolved. Preserve the prior ACIC denominator
quarantine independently; the two documents are different editions and scope.

## Calibration update discovered in the executed search

Weng et al., DOI [10.1093/ntr/ntag042](https://doi.org/10.1093/ntr/ntag042),
[PMID 41725392](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41725392/), is a separate, later
anabasine-excretion study recovered by S2. Its original PMC13389531 XML was also
retrieved and hashed outside tobac. Methods, Calculation of the Excretion Factor,
and Discussion specify a percentage-of-estimated-absorbed-dose factor, dependent
on assumed absorbed nicotine/anabasine and a literature nicotine-metabolite factor.
It cannot directly replace Wang's mass-per-cigarette factor: the quantities have
different dimensions and reference assumptions.

The original reports small-sample and demographic limitations and possible anabasine
contributions from vaping products. Treat it as an alternative calibration candidate,
not proof of perfect tobacco specificity or a universally applicable NZ factor.
Preserve dependence through shared calibration literature and authors' source studies;
new publication date does not establish independent calibration. No numerical factor
has been substituted into the existing engine or accepted parameter table.
